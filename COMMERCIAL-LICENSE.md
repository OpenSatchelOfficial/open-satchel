- **Commercial license: available on request.** Organizations that cannot or
  do not want to comply with AGPL obligations can purchase a commercial
  license that grants the same code under different terms. The commercial
  license removes the AGPL share-alike requirement and provides a written
  agreement suitable for procurement.

## Why a commercial license might be required

You should consider a commercial license if any of the following apply:

- Your organization's policy prohibits use of AGPL-licensed software.
- You want to embed the Open Satchel PDF engine inside a closed-source
  product, SDK, or hosted service without releasing your derivative work
  under AGPL.
- You want to distribute modified builds of Open Satchel without making
  the modifications public under AGPL.
- You operate Open Satchel as a network-accessible service for users
  outside your organization and do not want to provide source access to
  those users (the AGPL "remote network interaction" trigger).
- You need a written commercial agreement, signed terms, indemnity, an
  SLA, support obligations, or formal procurement paperwork that AGPL
  alone does not provide.
- You want signed release builds, a private update channel, or a
  contractually-defined source-access arrangement.

If none of these apply, AGPL-3.0 is sufficient and you do not need to
purchase anything. Personal, research, hobby, academic, and AGPL-compliant
internal company use is fully covered by the public AGPL license.

## What the commercial license includes

The exact terms are negotiated per agreement. Typical inclusions:

- A non-AGPL grant of rights covering the use case described above.
- Permission to embed, redistribute, white-label, or expose the engine
  through a closed-source product or service.
- Permission to keep modifications private.
- Optional add-ons: signed builds, prioritized bug fixes, support
  response targets, and procurement / vendor onboarding cooperation.

Pricing depends on scope, deployment shape, distribution rights, and
support expectations.

## How to start the conversation

Email **licensing@opensatchel.dev** with:

1. A short description of your intended use (internal tool, embedded
   product, hosted service, OEM redistribution, etc.).
2. The approximate scale (seats, end users, deployment count).
3. Whether you need support, signed builds, or specific procurement
   paperwork.
4. The jurisdictions involved if relevant for the agreement.

You will receive a written response with a draft scope and a price
range. Final commercial terms are documented in a signed agreement
before any commercial rights take effect.
